# Educational and interactive notebooks

Modern research and teaching require tools that are lightweight, accessible, and reproducible. In this short session, we will look at three complementary tools that lower the barrier to sharing and running Python code for interactive and reproducible computing:  

## Jupyter Books - This website

An open-source system for publishing computational documents and books.  
It combines Markdown, Jupyter Notebooks, and Sphinx to create interactive, reproducible teaching materials, research reports, and technical guides. You can try it out [in the online demo](https://zorbax.github.io/notebooks/).

## JupyterLite (with Pyodide)  

A lightweight Jupyter distribution that runs entirely in the browser, powered by WebAssembly through Pyodide. It requires no installation or server, making it ideal for workshops, classrooms, and quick demos. You can try it out [in the online demo](https://jupyter.org/try-jupyter/lab/) or in your own [Github Pages website](https://zorbax.github.io/notebooks/lab/).

## Marimo Notebooks  

A modern, reactive notebook system focused on reproducibility and simplicity.  
It uses plain Python files and a reactive execution model, making notebooks version-control friendly and automatically consistent. You can try it out [in the online demo](https://marimo.ai/try-wasm/), or in your own [Github Pages website](https://zorbax.github.io/notebooks/marimo/marimo.html).
